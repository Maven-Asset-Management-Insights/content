Planning answers how. Scheduling answers when. They sound like one job, and in a lot of Maximo shops one person does both. That's where the trouble starts.

A planner builds the work package: job steps, crafts, parts, tools, permits, and time estimates. Good planners work weeks ahead, and the best ones lean on [job plans](https://maven-asset-management-insights.github.io/content/glossary/#job-plan) so they aren't rebuilding the same information every time. A scheduler takes that ready work and fits it into the week, matching it to available crews, equipment downtime, and current priorities.

When one person carries both, the schedule always wins. It's due Friday. The planning can wait. So jobs go to the field half-prepped, crews stand around waiting on parts, and the schedule slips anyway. Over time your [planning accuracy](https://maven-asset-management-insights.github.io/content/glossary/#planning-accuracy) drops and your [backlog](https://maven-asset-management-insights.github.io/content/glossary/#maintenance-backlog-health) grows, and nobody can say exactly why.

You don't always need two new hires to fix it. You need clear ownership. Decide who owns how and who owns when, and give each role its own time on the calendar.
